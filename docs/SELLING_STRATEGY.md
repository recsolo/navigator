# Selling strategy

## Current call

Treat this product as a **Windows-first AI tool decision app for entrepreneurs and self-directed builders**.

Do not lead with vague language like "AI guidance engine." Lead with the outcome:

- stop wasting money on the wrong AI tools
- pick the right stack faster
- get a first workflow you can actually run

## Working positioning

**Navigator helps entrepreneurs choose the right AI tools and workflow without drowning in hype, bad subscriptions, or random YouTube advice.**

## Ideal first buyer

Target the buyer most likely to pay quickly:

- entrepreneur
- side hustler
- freelancer
- solo operator
- small business owner experimenting with AI

They are not looking for a research paper.
They want clarity, confidence, and a fast next move.

## Best first sales channels

### 1. Gumroad

Use as the first paid validation channel.

Why:
- fastest launch path
- simple file delivery
- easy pricing tests
- easy bundles
- good enough for early traction

### 2. Direct landing page

Use after the first paid proof.

Why:
- better trust
- stronger margins
- stronger brand ownership
- better upsell path

### 3. Etsy

Only use if the product is sold as a **bundle**, not just raw software.

Bundle shape:
- Windows desktop app
- quickstart PDF
- AI stack planning guide
- example workflows
- support/update promise

## Offer strategy

Do not sell the product as generic software.
Sell it as a decision shortcut.

### Core offer

**Navigator for AI Tool Buyers**

Promise:
- choose the right AI stack for your goal
- understand why it fits
- avoid wasting money on tools you do not need
- get a first workflow worth running

### Better niche variants

After the generic entrepreneur version, consider niche editions:
- Navigator for creators
- Navigator for freelancers
- Navigator for online sellers
- Navigator for small business owners

## Product strengths

- real pain point
- local-first angle
- installable app
- recommendation + workflow output
- optional OpenAI enhancement with fallback
- session history and preferences

## Product weaknesses to fix before selling

### 1. Naming inconsistency

The repo currently mixes "Navigator" and "Navagator".
This must be cleaned up.

### 2. Positioning language is too internal

Replace builder-centric language like:
- guidance engine
- local-first software for people overwhelmed by AI tool sprawl

With buyer-centric language like:
- stop wasting money on the wrong AI tools
- get your best AI stack faster
- pick the tools that fit your actual goal

### 3. No clear sales-facing asset set

Need:
- storefront copy
- product description
- feature bullets
- screenshots plan
- quickstart guide
- pricing test

### 4. Trust still has to be earned

Need to clearly explain:
- how recommendations are made
- when heuristic vs OpenAI mode is used
- that user data stays local by default
- who the product is for and not for

## Immediate execution order

1. Lock product positioning
2. Clean up naming and messaging across repo-facing assets
3. Create sales assets for Gumroad-first launch
4. Audit UX for trust and clarity issues
5. Package a clean release candidate
6. Decide pricing and bundle structure

## Pricing starting point

Use simple paid validation first.

Test one of these:
- $19 early access
- $29 launch price
- $49 bundle with guide/templates

Do not start with a subscription.

## What to optimize for

Optimize for:
- fast buyer understanding
- immediate perceived value
- clear trust signal
- low setup friction
- strong first-run result

Do not optimize for:
- enterprise complexity
- broad platform support right away
- feature sprawl
- overbuilt branding before proof

## Current working product statement

**Navigator is a Windows app that helps entrepreneurs pick the right AI tools for their goal, understand why they fit, and get a workflow to run first.**
